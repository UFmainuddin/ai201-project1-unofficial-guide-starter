Description: Flushing Queens rent prices, apartment types, sub-neighborhoods comparison, typical costs for students
---
Flushing, Queens is one of the more affordable neighborhoods in New York City for renters, especially compared to Manhattan, Brooklyn, or Long Island City. Here is a breakdown of typical rent prices and what to expect.

Typical monthly rent in Flushing (2024 estimates):
- Studio apartment: $1,300 - $1,600
- One-bedroom apartment: $1,500 - $1,900
- Two-bedroom apartment: $1,900 - $2,500
- Three-bedroom apartment: $2,400 - $3,200
- Room in shared apartment: $700 - $1,100

The cheapest options are typically in rooms in shared apartments, which is popular among students. A shared two-bedroom apartment split between two people can cost $950-$1,250 per person per month. A shared three-bedroom split three ways can be as low as $750-$900 per person.

Sub-neighborhood price differences:

Downtown Flushing (near Main Street / 7 train): Most expensive in the area but still affordable by NYC standards. Highest walkability and transit access. 1BR: $1,700-$2,000. Very close to food, shopping, and transportation.

Murray Hill (Flushing): About 15-20 blocks east of Main Street. Quieter residential feel, lower rents. Large Korean American community. 1BR: $1,500-$1,800. Good transit access via Q17 and LIRR Murray Hill station.

Broadway-Flushing: North of Main Street toward Northern Boulevard. Historical district with larger pre-war homes. Quieter, residential. 1BR: $1,500-$1,800. Less walkable to Main Street but accessible by bus.

Auburndale: Further east toward Bayside. Very residential, suburban feel. Lower density. 1BR: $1,400-$1,700. Served by LIRR Auburndale station. Longer commute to campus but quieter.

College Point: North of Flushing, toward the water. More isolated, less transit. Lower rents: 1BR around $1,400-$1,700. Limited bus service.

Tips for finding apartments:
- StreetEasy.com and Zillow.com have most formal listings
- Many landlords in Flushing post in WeChat groups or on local bulletin boards (not online)
- Walk the residential streets around Main Street and look for rental signs
- Ask at local businesses or community centers
- Facebook Marketplace NYC housing groups
- Avoid brokers who try to charge you a fee — in NYC, the landlord pays the broker fee

Costs beyond rent to budget for:
- Most Flushing apartments do NOT include utilities. Budget $50-100/month extra for electricity and gas.
- Internet: $40-70/month
- Metro card: $132/month for unlimited 30-day
- LIRR to campus (if applicable): additional monthly cost
