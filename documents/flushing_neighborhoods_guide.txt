Description: Guide to Flushing sub-neighborhoods for students: culture, amenities, pros and cons of each area
---
Flushing is a large and diverse neighborhood in northern Queens. It is one of the most densely populated neighborhoods in New York City. Here is a guide to the main sub-areas relevant to students looking for housing.

Downtown Flushing / Main Street area:
The commercial and cultural core of Flushing, centered on Main Street and Roosevelt Avenue. This is where the 7 train terminates and where the LIRR has a station. Extremely busy and diverse. The area has the highest concentration of Chinese, Korean, and South Asian restaurants, stores, and services in Queens. Great for students who want walkability and convenience. Downside: noisy, crowded, and the busiest streets can feel overwhelming. Best for students who like urban energy. The 7 train is right here.

Murray Hill (not to be confused with Manhattan's Murray Hill):
This is the area east of Main Street, roughly between 150th Street and 170th Street, and between Northern Boulevard and the LIRR tracks. It has a strong Korean American presence with Korean-owned businesses, restaurants, and cultural institutions. Quieter than downtown Flushing. The LIRR Murray Hill station provides fast access to Penn Station in Manhattan (about 20 minutes). Bus Q17 goes to Queens College campus. A good option for students who want a quieter setting with good transit.

Broadway-Flushing:
The area north of Northern Boulevard, between Main Street and Parsons Boulevard. This neighborhood has a historic district designation because of its large pre-war houses and tree-lined streets. The architecture is distinctive and the area is considered very desirable. It is quieter than downtown Flushing and has a more established residential feel. Rent is slightly lower than downtown but still accessible to Main Street by bus or a 15-minute walk.

Auburndale:
Further east, between Francis Lewis Boulevard and 210th Street. More suburban in character with detached homes and less commercial activity. The LIRR Auburndale station provides rail access but bus service to campus is limited. Best for students with cars or those who prefer a very quiet suburban environment. Lower rents than most of Flushing.

Kissena Park area:
Near the park of the same name, which is a medium-sized green space. This area borders Kew Gardens Hills, which is where Queens College is actually located. Living near Kissena Park means a very short commute to campus. The park is a pleasant place to study and relax. The area is mixed residential, with apartments and houses. Rents are comparable to Flushing or slightly lower.

Cultural life in Flushing:
Flushing is one of the most diverse places in the United States. The community is primarily Chinese American (Mandarin and Cantonese speaking), Korean American, and South Asian (Indian, Bangladeshi, Pakistani). There is also a growing Latin American community. This diversity means exceptional food, cultural events, and a welcoming environment for international students. Queens College itself has a very diverse student body that reflects the neighborhood.
