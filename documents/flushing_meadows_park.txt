Flushing Meadows-Corona Park is a large public park in the northern part of Queens, New York City. It is one of the largest parks in New York City, covering approximately 897 acres. The park is located just west of downtown Flushing and is accessible from the Main Street-Flushing subway station on the 7 train.

The park was the site of the 1939 and 1964 World's Fairs. The Unisphere, a large stainless steel globe, is a famous landmark from the 1964 World's Fair that still stands in the park.

Facilities and attractions in the park include: the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center, home of the US Open tennis tournament held every September; Citi Field, the baseball stadium for the New York Mets; the Queens Museum; the New York Hall of Science; the Queens Zoo; Meadow Lake with paddleboat rentals; multiple soccer and baseball fields; playgrounds and picnic areas.

The Queens Night Market takes place at the park seasonally and features food vendors from dozens of countries. It is one of the most diverse food markets in New York City and is free to attend.

For students living near Flushing, the park provides free green space, jogging paths, and recreation facilities. The park is easily walkable from Main Street Flushing. The LIRR and 7 train both provide access to the park area.

The park borders the neighborhoods of Flushing, Corona, and Willets Point. The Willets Point area near the park is under major redevelopment, with plans for thousands of new residential units and affordable housing. This development may increase housing density and availability near the park over the next several years.

Bus routes Q58, Q48, and Q23 serve the park area. From Queens College, students can take the Q25 to Flushing Main Street and then walk or take a connecting bus to the park.
